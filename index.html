ABOUT

Building models that survive being questioned

I'm a Biomedical Engineering graduate from VELS University, currently working as a Project Associate in the Office of IC&SR at IIT Madras. My work sits across medical imaging, biosignal processing, and machine learning — with a particular interest in whether a model's accuracy actually reflects the biology it claims to detect, or just artifacts in the data it was trained on.

That question runs through both of my current independent studies: one re-examines a facial-EMG wheelchair control dataset for real inter-subject variability, and the other audits deep learning models for ultrasound-based liver disease detection for hidden dataset confounding. I care as much about what a model gets wrong, and why, as what it gets right.

Outside formal research, I've worked across data analytics, embedded systems, and hospital biomedical engineering — which keeps the imaging and modelling work grounded in how it would actually need to function in a clinical or field setting.

RESEARCH INTERESTS

Where I focus

Four connected areas that show up across my published and ongoing work.

01

Medical Imaging

Working with ultrasound and biosignal data end-to-end — from acquisition and preprocessing (ROI cropping, CLAHE contrast enhancement) through to classification and evaluation, with an emphasis on catching dataset leakage before it inflates results.

02

Image Processing

Building preprocessing pipelines that remove acquisition artifacts — probe borders, depth text, scanner noise — so a model is left with signal rather than shortcuts, using OpenCV-based cropping and quality-metric auditing (brightness, contrast, sharpness, entropy).

03

Image Segmentation

Interested in segmentation as a way to localize where a model is actually looking — isolating anatomical regions of interest so downstream classification is anchored to biology rather than background texture.

04

Artificial Intelligence

Applying CNN architectures (EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-18) to biomedical classification tasks, paired with explainability tools like Grad-CAM and confounding-detection classifiers to test whether a model's accuracy is trustworthy, not just high.

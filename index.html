Quality-Aware Evaluation and Confounding Analysis of Deep Learning Models for Ultrasound-Based MASLD Detection

A quality-aware and explainable framework for evaluating deep learning models, image quality, data leakage, and dataset-level confounding in ultrasound-based MASLD detection.

Srinitha Sreekanth · Project Associate, Office of IC&SR · Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Madras

● Research Study Medical Imaging Deep Learning Ultrasound MASLD Explainable AI
01 · Overview

Overview

Deep learning models routinely report very high accuracy for detecting Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatotic Liver Disease (MASLD) from ultrasound — but high accuracy alone does not confirm that a model is actually learning disease-specific biomarkers rather than exploiting shortcuts present in the dataset.

This study develops a quality-aware and explainable framework to investigate that problem. EfficientNet-B0 and ResNet-18 were trained on approximately 4,500 cropped liver ultrasound images from public datasets, followed by image-quality analysis, leakage auditing, dataset-origin classification, and Grad-CAM-based interpretation.

Central Question: When a deep learning model achieves near-perfect diagnostic performance, is it actually learning disease-related information — or is it learning characteristics associated with the dataset itself?

02 · Methodology

Methods

Datasets LUS-7Seg healthy liver scans and a NAFLD/MASLD dataset, combined and standardized to grayscale 224 × 224 B-mode images.
Leakage Auditing MD5 hashing for exact duplicate detection and perceptual hashing (pHash) for near-duplicate identification, with patient-level splits wherever possible.
Preprocessing Automated ROI cropping using OpenCV to remove probe interfaces, depth text, and acoustic cone borders, followed by CLAHE contrast enhancement.
Image Quality Brightness, contrast, sharpness, and entropy were extracted for individual ultrasound images.
Deep Learning EfficientNet-B0 and ResNet-18 were fine-tuned for binary classification between normal and MASLD images using AdamW and binary cross-entropy.
Explainability Grad-CAM was used to visualize model activation and investigate whether attention was focused on liver parenchyma or other image regions.
Confounding Analysis A separate classifier was trained to predict dataset origin rather than disease status, providing a direct test for source-related confounding.

04 · Results

Model Performance

Both deep learning models achieved very high apparent diagnostic performance on the evaluated dataset.

Metric EfficientNet-B0 ResNet-18
Accuracy 99.53% 97.63%
AUC 99.99% 99.12%
Precision 99.57% 95.31%
Recall 99.15% 98.73%
F1 Score 99.36% 96.89%

Data Leakage Audit

The initial dataset contained duplicate and near-duplicate images. The audit identified 93 exact MD5 duplicates and 85 near-duplicates using pHash .

After removal, the resulting clean dataset contained approximately 4,458 images distributed across training, validation, and testing splits with zero cross-split overlap.

Quality-Stratified Evaluation

EfficientNet-B0 maintained approximately 99.53% accuracy across low-, medium-, and high-sharpness groups, indicating that the observed performance was not strongly dependent on the measured image-quality variation.

05 · Key Finding

The Accuracy Is Confounded

Principal Observation

Near-perfect diagnostic accuracy does not necessarily imply disease-specific learning.

A ResNet-18 model trained only to predict dataset origin — without using the disease label — achieved 99.11% accuracy with an AUC of 1.0000 .

Because dataset origin is strongly associated with disease status in this experimental setup, the result indicates substantial source-level confounding.

Grad-CAM analysis further showed diffuse activation across texture-rich image regions rather than a consistently localized focus on liver parenchyma.

MASLD images also demonstrated systematically different baseline image properties, including lower brightness, lower contrast, lower sharpness, and higher entropy than healthy images. These differences may arise from the underlying datasets rather than the disease itself.

06 · Significance

Why This Matters

Near-perfect accuracy in a single-source or poorly audited multi-source dataset can appear to represent a clinical breakthrough while actually reflecting dataset-specific acquisition characteristics.

This work demonstrates the importance of looking beyond accuracy and incorporating:

  • Data leakage auditing
  • Image-quality analysis
  • Quality-stratified evaluation
  • Dataset-origin classification
  • Explainable AI using Grad-CAM
  • External and multi-center validation

The framework provides a practical approach for identifying potential shortcut learning before a medical imaging model is considered for real-world deployment.

07 · Limitations

Limitations

  • Healthy and MASLD images originated from separate datasets, creating entanglement between disease status and acquisition source.
  • Patient-level identifiers were not consistently available across both datasets.
  • No external multi-center validation was performed in the current study.
  • Disease severity and fibrosis staging were not evaluated.
  • The findings demonstrate a risk of shortcut learning and source confounding, rather than proving that the models learned no genuine disease biomarkers.
08 · Future Directions

Future Work

Future investigations can extend the framework toward stronger clinical validation and more robust model evaluation.

  • Demographic subgroup evaluation including sex, age, and BMI.
  • Calibration analysis using Brier score and Expected Calibration Error (ECE).
  • Domain adaptation and dataset harmonization.
  • Multi-center external validation.
  • More robust evaluation of model attention and disease-specific biomarkers.
